### CE: ce.l1.does-withdrawal-reach-content

- Status: PROPOSED, awaiting business authority. Nothing downstream may answer it.
- Level: L1. It asks how far `L1.hole.deletion-reversal` reaches, and it exists because the coordinator answered that question in SQL by mistake and a review caught it.
- How it arose, recorded because the mechanism is the point: a projection reviewer found that the carry-forward windows had no withdrawal boundary, so the first real withdrawal row would carry `tax_treatment`, owner and tier onto the withdrawal statement and trip three blocking audits against the model's own SQL. That part was a genuine defect and is fixed by suppressing a withdrawal statement's own output. The coordinator then went further and instructed both engines to make a withdrawal a hard carry-forward boundary, on the argument that a pre-withdrawal fact surviving onto a LATER statement is exactly the record resuming, which `L1.hole.deletion-reversal` forbids. Both Developers checked that argument against the model text before implementing it and agreed with it. It was still wrong.
- What the next review found, by executing the on-disk audit files against a hand-built withdrawal population on both engines: the boundary contradicts five selected must-hold invariants. `inv.account_statement_has_content` and `inv.account_tax_treatment_matches_producing_source` fire on a post-withdrawal CLOSEACCT; `inv.customer_statement_has_content` and `inv.customer_tier_matches_producing_source` on a post-withdrawal INACT; `inv.account_owner_matches_producing_source` on a post-withdrawal constructed row. The model text for the three value invariants says the value equals the value carried by the immediately preceding statement, with no boundary, and two of them carry no withdrawal exception at all.
- Why the coordinator's argument failed: `logical.*.is_current`'s reversal-foreclosure already implements `L1.hole.deletion-reversal` completely for this job. A record has no standing from its withdrawal forward, and for a job that answers standing questions, that IS what "no report after a withdrawal resumes the record" means. Extending the same hole from currency to CONTENT is not a more careful deferral; it answers the open question in the not-resuming direction, in SQL, which is the one thing the hole forbids.
- The question for the business: a record is withdrawn, and a later report about it arrives. `L1.hole.deletion-reversal` reserves what that later report means. Does the reservation reach only whether the record has standing again -- which the projection already answers by refusing to make any post-withdrawal statement current -- or does it also reach what facts such a statement carries? Concretely: a customer is withdrawn while their tier stands at Gold, and a later report changes only their status. Does the statement that report produces carry tier Gold forward across the withdrawal, or does tier come out unknown because the value did not survive the withdrawal?
- Three readings are live and this document does not choose among them. (1) The hole reaches currency only: a post-withdrawal statement is never current and never an as-of answer, but its facts carry forward normally, because `L1.omitted-facts-stand` says a fact stands at its last stated value and the withdrawal did not restate it. This is what both projections do today and what the five invariants as written require. (2) The hole reaches content too: nothing survives a withdrawal, so a post-withdrawal statement's unmentioned facts are unknown rather than carried. This is what the coordinator implemented and what the five invariants would have to be rewritten to permit. (3) A report after a withdrawal is itself reportable for review, the way a trade on a closed account is, because only the brokerage can say whether the withdrawal or the later report is the error -- in which case the carry-forward question may not need answering at all.
- What is NOT authorized by this document: answering it. Both engines have been returned to reading (1), which is the behaviour their selected invariants state, and no audit was adjusted to fit. A Developer told to make the five audits pass would have repartitioned them and answered the hole in SQL, which the reviewer named and refused.
- Deterministic assertion, once answered: on a labeled constructed scenario carrying a withdrawal followed by a later report that omits a standing fact, both engines agree on whether that fact is carried or unknown, and whichever way the business answers, the five invariants above and the carry-forward derivations say the same thing as each other.
- Adjacent, and not this document's to raise: whether a withdrawn record can be reported again at all, which is `L1.hole.deletion-reversal`'s original question and stays open regardless of how this one is answered.
- Proposed by: the coordinator, from a projection reviewer's finding against the coordinator's own instruction.
