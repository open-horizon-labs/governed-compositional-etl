### CE: ce.l1.deletion-withdraws

- Status: accepted 2026-09-17 (business authority); clause `L1.deletion-withdraws` added, clause `L1.current-version` amended, hole `L1.hole.deletions` closed, hole `L1.hole.deletion-reversal` opened
- Level: L1, asked by the chain itself. The positions Developer stated a question for authority rather than deciding in SQL: a holding pair whose only reports are all D-flagged currently produces no `logical.holding_change` row at all, because with no I, U, or historical candidate the before and after quantities cannot be filled. It recorded that its no-row behavior was one behavior consistent with `L1.hole.deletions`, not an answer to it, and asked whether such a pair should never appear, appear with quantities held at some stated value, or be treated as an error.
- Input and simulation context: `chain/anchors/sources-v1.json` names `D` in the `cdc_flag` vocabulary and says in as many words that its meaning is `L1.hole.deletions`, so the anchor deferred the question rather than answering it. The received TPC-DI records carry no D-flagged row anywhere (account, customer, trade, or holding), so nothing in the received data forced the question and nothing in it can test the answer: a labeled constructed scenario is required, withdrawing a record the brokerage already has.
- Projection output before the answer: ownership-history ignored the flag entirely -- a D-flagged account or customer report produced a dated statement exactly as an update would, and the withdrawn record stayed current. trade-lifecycle and positions each filtered D out of their candidate reports, so the three jobs disagreed about what a deletion meant while every audit passed.
- Question for the business: what does deleting a record mean for history, for an as-of answer, and for what already followed from it?
- Answer (business authority, 2026-09-17): a deletion withdraws the brokerage's assertion of the record from the moment the report states, and erases nothing. Earlier statements stand and earlier as-of questions answer unchanged. From the withdrawal forward the record has no standing, so an as-of question at or after it has no answer rather than an older one. A withdrawal creates nothing and neither opens nor closes an account. It does not reach back through what already followed: a holding or position that followed a trade before its withdrawal stands as history. A thing whose only reports are withdrawals was never held at all, so nothing comes into being from it and it contributes to no position.
- Corrected output or behavior: the positions question is answered with its first option -- a D-only pair never appears -- but now as a rule rather than as an accident of nullability, so an audit can hold it. ownership-history must stop treating a withdrawal as an ordinary statement. `L1.current-version` is amended from "exactly one current statement" to "at most one", because a withdrawn record has none; this is the first time a clause has had to be weakened by another clause rather than extended, and ownership-history's job feedback is amended with it.
- Adjacent behavior not authorized: deciding what a report received after a withdrawal means. That is `L1.hole.deletion-reversal` and nothing downstream may answer it by guessing. Also not authorized: deleting or hiding the earlier statements of a withdrawn record, recomputing a position backwards, or reading a withdrawal as a closure.
- Tempting wrong repair: implementing a withdrawal as a physical delete, or as a statement whose standing facts are null. The first erases history the clause says stands; the second leaves the record current with empty facts, which is an answer where the clause says there is none.
- Deterministic assertion: on a labeled constructed scenario that withdraws an account the received records already carry, that account has no current statement and no as-of answer at or after the withdrawal, every statement before it answers exactly as it did without the withdrawal, and the trades and holdings already attributed to its earlier statements are byte-identical to their values before the scenario was added. On both engines.
