### CE: ce.l1.withdrawal-reaches-standing

- Status: accepted 2026-09-17 (business authority); `L1.deletion-withdraws` extended, `L1.owner-standing` extended, `L1.constructed-scenarios` given one narrow exception; `L1.hole.deletions` stays closed
- Level: L1, raised by the cache adjudicator. Asked to decide keep or invalidate for ten groups after the business answered `L1.hole.deletions`, a capable model split them seven to two and then reported that three of its own verdicts rested on readings the clause did not supply. The findings were worth more than the verdicts, which is why they are archived as a counterexample rather than as review notes.
- Input and simulation context: `ce-withdrawn-account-v1` withdraws account 624 after its two real statements and reports a holding pair over trades 353232 and 353232's successor as a deletion only. Verified against the projections rather than assumed.
- Projection output before the answer: three separate behaviors, each checked. `trade-lifecycle`'s `valid_cdc_rows` admits `D` while only `latest_outcome` narrows to `I` and `U`, so a trade reported only as a deletion today receives a persisted row with a placement, a frozen owner, and a null outcome -- the direct opposite of the clause's last sentence, and the sharpest violation the answer creates. `positions` produces no holding change for a deletion-only pair, the required outcome, reached by the `D` row supplying no candidate quantity rather than by any rule, so nothing holds it. `ownership-history` shows the withdrawal of an account not at all, and not because it reads the flag and ignores it: it reads only `raw.customer_mgmt_action` and never the CDC sources, because every one of its declared CDC handoffs is deferred under `L1.hole.change-effective-time`.
- Questions for the business, and the answers (business authority, 2026-09-17):
  1. An account stands while the customer that owns it is withdrawn. `L1.owner-standing` says the account's standing includes its owner's; `L1.deletion-withdraws` says the owner has none. Neither clause yielded. **Answer:** withdrawing a customer withdraws the standing of every account it owns, from that same moment, because an account's standing includes its owner's and there is no owner standing left to include. An account's as-of answer is never half an answer with the owner's part missing, which was the repair `ce-withdrawn-account-v1` named as tempting and wrong. And because a customer that still owns accounts is one the brokerage may not have meant to withdraw, the withdrawal is reported for review, using the mechanism `L1.closed-account-activity` introduced: the chain says it back rather than deciding it.
  2. The clause is written in as-of language, but a trade and a holding change are not asked as of a moment -- single current records, no effective_from -- so "from its moment forward the record has no standing" had no literal reading for them. **Answer:** a withdrawn trade keeps its record and is marked withdrawn, it is not among the trades the brokerage asserts, and everything that already followed from it stands as history. This is projectable without making a trade as-of-able, and it preserves both "erases nothing" and "does not reach back".
  3. A rule about a record whose only report is a withdrawal cannot be exercised, because testing it needs a scenario naming an identifier the records do not carry, which `L1.constructed-scenarios` forbids. **Answer:** one exception, for withdrawals only. A scenario may carry a withdrawal of a customer, account, or trade the records do not carry, because under `L1.deletion-withdraws` such a report asserts nothing and brings nothing into being. A scenario still may not name an unknown identifier in any other kind of report.
- Corrected output or behavior: `sg.owner-standing` gains a projectable rule where it had none. A D-only trade must be claimed absent, which `sg.trade-shape` and `sg.trade-identity` must now express and which `ce-withdrawn-account-v1` could not previously test. The adjudicator's keep for `trade-lifecycle/sg.outcome` is confirmed rather than revisited, because answer 2 takes the "stands as history" reading its keep rested on.
- What remains held, and is not answered here: the account and customer halves of `L1.deletion-withdraws` cannot be discharged by any re-projection, because `ownership-history` does not read the sources carrying the flag while `L1.hole.change-effective-time` stays open. Answering one hole made visible that the case sits behind a second. This is recorded, not resolved: nothing downstream may read a withdrawal into `ownership-history` by guessing what the deferred handoffs would do.
- Adjacent behavior not authorized: what a report received after a withdrawal means, which stays `L1.hole.deletion-reversal`; whether a withdrawn account's own trades are reportable the way a closed account's are; deciding which of a customer withdrawal and its standing accounts is the error.
- Tempting wrong repair: answering finding 1 by letting the account keep answering with a null owner half, which satisfies the letter of `L1.owner-standing` and empties it of meaning. Or answering finding 3 by widening `L1.constructed-scenarios` generally, which would let a scenario invent a received record and is the one thing that clause exists to prevent.
- Deterministic assertion: after the L2 recompiles this demands, on both engines -- a customer withdrawal leaves no account of that customer with a standing at or after its moment, and is reported for review; a trade reported only as a withdrawal has no row; a trade withdrawn after earlier reports keeps its row marked withdrawn with its holdings unchanged; and the account half of `ce-withdrawn-account-v1` remains unexercised, with the group that would answer it naming `L1.hole.change-effective-time` in its gap.
- Proposed by: the cache adjudicator, from its own verdicts; carried up by the coordinator.
- Approved or rejected by: approved, business authority (2026-09-17).
